# Customer step-up answers

Sentinel Bank fraud operations, internal policy (demo document written for this project).

## The three questions
When a payment is held, the customer can confirm it with a passkey and three questions: is someone on a call asking you to make this payment; were you promised a refund, prize, job or investment return, or told your account is at risk; do you know this payee.

## Reading the answers
Yes to the call question or to the promise question matches a known scam pattern: the payment stays held, the customer sees a warning, and the payment goes to the top of the queue. Not knowing the payee is common for merchants and is not a scam sign alone.

## Coached customers
Scammers tell victims what to answer. Answers can only make a decision stricter: if coaching signals were seen during the payment, or the payee is CONFIRMED on the ledger, the payment stays held for an analyst even if every answer was safe. Treat "no call" from a customer whose payment showed an active call as a strong scam sign, not as reassurance.

## One attempt
A customer can answer once per payment, so a scammer on the line cannot coach a second attempt.
