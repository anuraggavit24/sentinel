# Scam patterns we see

Sentinel Bank fraud operations, internal reference (demo document written for this project).

## Digital arrest and impersonation of authorities
The victim gets a call or video call from someone posing as police, a court, customs or a regulator. They are told they are under investigation and must transfer money to a "safe account" to clear their name, and must stay on the call. Signs: an active call or screen sharing during the payment, a large first payment to a new payee, the customer answering yes to "someone on a call asked you to pay". No authority asks anyone to move money to prove innocence.

## Account blocked or KYC update
The victim is told their account or SIM will be blocked unless they update KYC through a link or a remote-support app. The scammer then sees the screen or gets codes and moves money. Signs: screen sharing, a new device, a SIM swap shortly before the payment, many small payments in quick succession.

## Refund, reversal or wrong transfer
The victim is told money was sent to them by mistake, or that a refund is waiting, and is asked to "return" it or to approve a collect request. Signs: the customer believes they are receiving money, a payee they do not know, the customer answering yes to "were you promised a refund".

## Job, task and investment scams
The victim is offered paid online tasks or high returns, receives small payouts at first, then is asked to pay larger "deposits" or "fees" to withdraw. Signs: repeated payments to new payees, amounts growing over days, several of our customers paying the same payee.

## Mule accounts
Money from victims is moved through accounts opened or rented for the purpose. A payee that several customers of different banks have tried to pay, often in a short time, is a strong mule signal. The consortium ledger exists so that banks see these reports from each other.

## Account takeover
The scammer controls the customer's account from their own device, often after a SIM swap. Signs: a new device together with a SIM swap or a failed device integrity check, a typing rhythm unlike the customer's, and payments to new payees. Such sessions are sandboxed.
